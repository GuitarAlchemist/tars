# Bayesian Grammar Induction in TARS: An Audit of the Probabilistic Grammar Stack

## Abstract

TARS advertises a three-force "probabilistic grammar" stack: EBNF constrained decoding (Force 1), Beta-Binomial Bayesian weights (Force 2), and replicator dynamics (Force 3). This audit, adversarially verified against the live codebase and on-disk state, finds that the Bayesian layer is largely disconnected and statistically degenerate. The Beta-Binomial update starts from zero prior pseudo-count mass, so a rule's first observation collapses its posterior mean to exactly 0.0 or 1.0; the configured `PriorSuccessRate` is dead code. The "success" observation fed into the update is the Grammar Governor's own approval decision rather than any execution outcome, making the posterior self-confirming. The posterior, once computed, never propagates into the `Weight` field that `classifyWeighted` actually ranks by — promotions are ordered by a stale softmax of initial criteria scores. Selection is pure exploitation everywhere: the one stochastic selector over these weights, `selectWeighted`, has zero production callers, and the bandit in `PatternSelector` softmaxes posterior *means* at temperature 1, discarding all count information and compressing a 195/195 versus 0/5 live outcome record into roughly a 0.03 score difference. The promotion staircase is threshold gating, not Bayesian model selection: an MDL-like `CompressionRatio` is computed during distillation but never consulted by any promotion decision, in contrast to Stolcke–Omohundro model merging and DreamCoder library compression, where promotion is exactly a posterior/description-length gain test. Twelve findings were confirmed by two independent review passes; none were merely plausible, and none were refuted. Concrete fixes are ranked at the end: Beta(1,1)-seeded pseudo-count priors, execution-grounded observations, Thompson sampling over Beta posteriors, and an MDL gain criterion for staircase promotion.

## Background

The theoretical frame for this unit is Bayesian grammar induction: the problem of learning a grammar (or a library of reusable abstractions) from data by maximizing posterior probability P(G)·P(D|G), established by Horning (1969) and made operational in two canonical forms. Stolcke and Omohundro (1994) induce probabilistic grammars by *model merging*: candidate state merges are accepted only when the Bayesian posterior of the grammar given the data improves. DreamCoder (Ellis et al., 2021) frames library growth as *compression*: a subexpression is promoted into the library only when doing so reduces the total description length of the library plus all programs expressed in it. Both traditions share one invariant: a structural promotion is justified by a quantitative gain in posterior probability or description length, computed from observed data. A probabilistic context-free grammar, the object Force 2 nominally approximates, additionally requires per-nonterminal normalization — Σ P(A→ξ) = 1 over the competing expansions of each nonterminal A (Booth & Thompson, 1973) — with Dirichlet priors on exactly those per-nonterminal multinomials in the Bayesian treatment (Johnson, Griffiths & Goldwater, 2007).

On the decision-theoretic side, the relevant standard is the Beta-Bernoulli bandit: maintain a Beta(α, β) posterior per arm, seeded with proper prior pseudo-counts, and select either by Thompson sampling — drawing θ ~ Beta(α, β) per arm and taking the argmax of the draws (Russo et al., 2018) — or by a UCB-style optimism bonus (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi & Fischer, 2002). For non-stationary rewards, discounting the effective counts per *time step* across all arms, including un-pulled ones, is the established device (Garivier & Moulines, 2008).

TARS implements recognizable fragments of this machinery — `WeightedGrammar.bayesianUpdate` (Beta-Binomial form), `PatternSelector.banditScores` (Laplace-smoothed Beta means), `ReplicatorDynamics` (evolutionary game dynamics), `GrammarDistillation.CompressionRatio` (an MDL-flavored statistic) — spread across `v2/src/Tars.Evolution` and `v2/src/Tars.Cortex`, with live state on disk at `~/.tars/promotion/weights.json` (grammar-rule weights) and `~/.tars/pattern_outcomes.json` (231 execution records at audit time). The audit question is whether these fragments compose into the Bayesian learner the architecture claims, or merely coexist. Every finding below was checked against the source at specific line anchors and, where applicable, against the live JSON state; each was independently confirmed by two adversarial review passes.

## Findings

### 1. The Beta-Binomial prior is degenerate and the configured prior is dead code

**Claim** (`degenerate-beta-prior`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). `WeightedGrammar.bayesianUpdate` has zero prior pseudo-counts for fresh rules, so the first observation drives the posterior mean to exactly 1.0 or 0.0, and the configured `PriorSuccessRate = 0.5` is never referenced by any update path.

The update computes α = priorRate·effectiveCount + 1{success} and β = (1−priorRate)·effectiveCount + 1{failure}, with effectiveCount = priorCount·decay (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:94-106`). New rules are created with `SelectionCount = 0` (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:131`), so effectiveCount = 0 and the first update yields α+β = 1 and a posterior mean of exactly 1.0 or 0.0. `updateWeight` passes `rule.SuccessRate` and `rule.SelectionCount`, never `config.PriorSuccessRate` (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:141-142`); project-wide grep finds `PriorSuccessRate` only at its definition and default (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:59-66`). The live state confirms the collapse: all five `ga.*` entries in `~/.tars/promotion/weights.json` sit at `SuccessRate` exactly 1 with `SelectionCount` 4. Under a proper Laplace Beta(1,1) prior — which `PatternSelector.banditScores` does use, at `v2/src/Tars.Cortex/PatternSelector.fs:252-254` — one success would yield a posterior mean of 2/3, not 1.0 (Russo et al., 2018).

### 2. The success signal is the governor's own decision — a circular update

**Claim** (`success-signal-circular`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). The "success observation" fed to the Beta-Binomial update in the promotion pipeline is the Grammar Governor's own Approve decision, not an execution outcome, so the posterior measures agreement with a deterministic rule-based gate rather than empirical pattern success.

`PromotionPipeline.run` sets `let success = match decision with Approve _ -> true | _ -> false` and feeds it to `WeightedGrammar.updateWeight` (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/PromotionPipeline.fs:354-359`). That decision comes from `GrammarGovernor.evaluate`, a deterministic threshold function of criteria (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarGovernor.fs:27-52`), and those criteria are mostly defaulted: `validate` is called with `llmAssessment = None` (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/PromotionPipeline.fs:333`), and `MoreReadable`/`ComposesCleanly` default to true. Actual execution outcomes exist — `PatternOutcomeStore` (`v2/src/Tars.Cortex/PatternSelector.fs:80-86`, backed by `~/.tars/pattern_outcomes.json` with 231 records) and `ReplicatorDynamics` fitness — but neither flows into this update. The only other `updateWeight` caller is the MCP `grammar_update` tool, which takes caller-supplied success and is a manual entry point, not an execution-outcome route. The observation likelihood and the decision policy are conflated: the posterior can only ever learn what the gate already decided.

### 3. The posterior never reaches the ranking it is supposed to inform

**Claim** (`posterior-never-reaches-ranking`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). `updateWeight` updates `SuccessRate` and `Confidence` but never the `Weight` field, and `classifyWeighted` ranks candidates by `Weight` alone — so the Bayesian posterior is computed, persisted, and then ignored by the only consumer that orders promotions.

`updateWeight` returns the rule with `SuccessRate`, `Confidence`, `SelectionCount`, and `LastUpdated` changed; `Weight` is untouched (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:143-147`). `Weight` is set once at creation from a softmax of criteria-score logits (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:119-128`), and `classifyWeighted` sorts solely by it (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/PromotionPipeline.fs:179-183`). The live state exhibits the divergence directly: `hypothesis_test_loop` has `SuccessRate` 0.745 but `Weight` 0.372 frozen since creation, and the `ga.*` rules with `SuccessRate` 1.0 sit at weights 0.19–0.21, *below* never-updated rules at 0.31–0.32. The MCP `grammar_update` response even reports `OldWeight`/`NewWeight` fields that are always equal.

### 4. Every live selection path is pure exploitation; no Thompson sampling exists

**Claim** (`pure-exploitation-no-thompson`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). All live selection paths over the Bayesian state are deterministic argmax/sort over posterior point estimates; the stochastic selector `WeightedGrammar.selectWeighted` has zero production callers, and no code performs Thompson sampling or applies a UCB exploration bonus outside of MCTS tree search.

`classifyWeighted` is `List.sortByDescending` on point weights (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/PromotionPipeline.fs:179-183`); `HistoryAwareSelector.Recommend` is `List.maxBy` on combined scores (`v2/src/Tars.Cortex/PatternSelector.fs:382-383`); `selectWeighted` (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:150-165`) is referenced only by tests. Grep for Thompson sampling in `v2/src` returns nothing; UCB1 exists only inside `v2/src/Tars.Evolution/MctsSolver.fs:16-22`, scoped to tree search. One caveat surfaced during review: `ResearchWeights.sampleMethod` *is* a live stochastic roulette selector (called from `ResearchCycle.fs:35-36`), so "the only stochastic selector" is a slight overstatement — but it samples research methods over heuristic weights, not grammar rules or patterns over Beta posteriors, so the substantive claim about the posterior-consuming paths stands. Thompson sampling — drawing θ ~ Beta(α, β) per arm and taking the argmax of draws — is the standard remedy (Russo et al., 2018; Auer et al., 2002 for the UCB alternative) and is nearly a one-line change given the (α, β) pairs already computed at `v2/src/Tars.Cortex/PatternSelector.fs:252-254`.

### 5. Softmax over posterior means discards uncertainty and compresses 231 outcomes into noise

**Claim** (`softmax-of-means-discards-uncertainty`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). `PatternSelector.banditScores` softmaxes Beta posterior *means* at temperature 1.0, which (a) discards all posterior uncertainty (the α+β magnitude), and (b) since means lie in [0,1], bounds the max/min probability ratio at e ≈ 2.72; after the 0.2 multiplier in `combineScores`, the bandit contributes at most ~0.03–0.06 of score separation.

`banditScores` computes means α/(α+β) and then exp/sum with no count term (`v2/src/Tars.Cortex/PatternSelector.fs:247-263`); `combineScores` adds 0.2 × banditBoost against heuristic scores spanning 0.2–0.8 (`v2/src/Tars.Cortex/PatternSelector.fs:354-370`). The live data makes the compression concrete: `~/.tars/pattern_outcomes.json` records ChainOfThought at 195/195 successes (mean ≈ 0.995), ReAct at 0/5 (mean ≈ 0.143), TreeOfThoughts at 0/3. Softmax over these means gives roughly 0.30 versus 0.13; scaled by 0.2, the difference is ≈ 0.035 — smaller than a single heuristic keyword match (0.4–0.5). An arm with 1/1 successes and an arm with 195/196 receive nearly identical treatment because the count information is destroyed at the mean; the review pass additionally noted that the many one-off Custom arms in the live store dilute the softmax further, making the effect even smaller than claimed (Russo et al., 2018, on why posterior sampling preserves exactly the count-dependent uncertainty that point-estimate policies discard).

### 6. `ResearchWeights.bayesianAdjust` is mislabeled — its docstring is false

**Claim** (`researchweights-mislabeled-bayesian`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). `ResearchWeights.bayesianAdjust` is documented as "Beta-Binomial posterior similar to WeightedGrammar.bayesianUpdate" but is actually a fixed-step heuristic — weight × 0.95 + 0.05 on success, − 0.03 on failure — with no likelihood, no counts, and no posterior.

The body at `v2/src/Tars.Evolution/ResearchWeights.fs:101-105` is `let adjustment = if success then 0.05 else -0.03` followed by `max 0.01 (currentWeight * decayFactor + adjustment)`. This is an exponentially-weighted additive update, not conjugate updating; it cannot represent certainty, and its fixed point depends only on the success/failure ratio through the asymmetric step sizes. Either the docstring should be corrected, or the update replaced with true per-method Beta counters — in which case the `sampleMethod` roulette at `ResearchWeights.fs:86-99` would become Thompson sampling essentially for free.

### 7. The running-average update overweights recent batches by a factor of ~k

**Claim** (`running-average-bias`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). `PromotionPipeline.extract` computes `AverageScore` by consing the historical mean onto the new batch scores and averaging, which weights the entire history as a single observation.

The code is verbatim `let allScores = existing.AverageScore :: (group |> List.map (fun a -> a.Score))` then `List.average` (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/PromotionPipeline.fs:135-136`). With 10 historical tasks at 0.9 and one new batch of 5 at 0.2, the correct pooled mean is 0.667, but this computes (0.9 + 5×0.2)/6 = 0.317. The bias is not cosmetic: `AverageScore` feeds the `ImprovesPlanning` criterion threshold (> 0.6) at `v2/src/Tars.Evolution/PromotionPipeline.fs:208` and the weight-creation logits at `v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:120`, so it perturbs both criteria scoring and initial `Weight`. The standard fix is a stored (sum, count) pair or the incremental mean formula; alternatively, per-task scores could be recomputed exactly since `TaskIds` are already retained.

### 8. The promotion staircase is threshold gating, not Bayesian model selection or MDL

**Claim** (`staircase-not-bayesian-model-selection`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). The staircase (Implementation → Helper → Builder → DslClause → GrammarRule) promotes on count thresholds (≥ 3 occurrences) and boolean criteria counts (≥ 6/8 approve, < 4 reject), with no marginal likelihood and no description-length comparison — and the MDL-like `CompressionRatio` computed during distillation is never consulted by any promotion decision.

`GrammarGovernor.evaluate` decides purely on `MinOccurrences`, overlap, rollback presence, and criteria-count thresholds (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarGovernor.fs:27-52`). `GrammarDistillation` computes `CompressionRatio` = traceTokens/productionTokens (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarDistillation.fs:206-209`), but `toWeightedRule` ignores it (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarDistillation.fs:284-296`), and `SuggestedLevel` uses only successRate/count thresholds (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarDistillation.fs:218-220`). One nuance from review: `CompressionRatio` *is* consumed by `GrammarMlBridge` as an ML feature, but that path only adjusts `SuccessRate`/`Confidence`, which — per Finding 3 — never reach `GrammarGovernor.evaluate` or `classifyWeighted`'s ordering; the central assertion that no promotion decision consults compression evidence survives. The contrast with the literature is sharp: Stolcke–Omohundro (1994) merge grammar states only when P(grammar|data) improves; DreamCoder (Ellis et al., 2021) promotes a subexpression into the library only when it reduces total description length; Grünwald (1996) and Horning (1969) give the MDL and Bayesian foundations respectively. TARS makes promotion decisions with none of the compression evidence it already computes.

### 9. There is no PCFG: the three forces are architecturally uncoupled

**Claim** (`not-a-pcfg`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). Despite the "PCFG-like weights" framing, TARS has no probabilistic context-free grammar in the technical sense: weights attach to whole patterns, normalization is per promotion level rather than per nonterminal over competing productions, the EBNF files carry no probability annotations, and the weights never reach the constrained decoder.

`normalizeByLevel` groups by `PromotionLevel` and normalizes within level (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:168-174`), whereas a PCFG requires Σ P(A→ξ) = 1 over the alternative expansions of each nonterminal A (Booth & Thompson, 1973), with Dirichlet priors on exactly these per-nonterminal multinomials in the Bayesian setting (Johnson et al., 2007). Grep over `v2/grammars/*.ebnf` (e.g., `v2/grammars/wot.ebnf:1`) for weight or probability annotations returns nothing beyond numeric-literal character classes. `ConstrainedDecoding.fs` mentions `WeightedGrammar` only in an architecture comment — "Layer 2: Probabilistic policy → WeightedGrammar steers preferences" (`v2/src/Tars.Llm/ConstrainedDecoding.fs:12`) — with no code path passing weights into guided decoding. No Dirichlet-multinomial machinery exists anywhere in `v2/src`. Force 2 therefore lives beside Force 1 rather than steering it.

### 10. The ML "prior" is linear opinion pooling, not conjugate prior mass

**Claim** (`ml-prior-linear-pooling-not-conjugate`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). `GrammarMlBridge.applyPredictivePrior` implements the ix ML prediction as a confidence-weighted average of point estimates, not a Bayesian prior: the blend overwrites `SuccessRate` without adjusting effective counts, so subsequent `bayesianUpdate` calls treat the ML-informed rate as if it carried the rule's full observational history.

The blend sets `SuccessRate = pred*conf + rate*(1-conf)` and `Confidence = max(conf*0.5, old)` while leaving `SelectionCount` untouched (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarMlBridge.fs:222-236`); the next `bayesianUpdate` then reconstitutes α/β as blendedRate × oldCount × decay (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:101-103`), conflating prior strength with data strength. The conjugate-correct encoding of an external predictor with mean m and confidence c is pseudo-counts α₀ = c·k·m, β₀ = c·k·(1−m) added to the rule's counts (Russo et al., 2018) — expressible in the existing (rate, count) parametrization, but not what the blend does. Additionally, `parsePrediction`'s "confidence" is just the max class probability of a random-forest classifier (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarMlBridge.fs:199-218`), an uncalibrated quantity. The function is live, called from the bridge's main path.

### 11. The decay device is half right: per-update forgetting inverts recency semantics

**Claim** (`decay-forgetting-half-correct`, confidence: medium, confirmed ×2). The exponential decay in `bayesianUpdate` (effectiveCount × 0.95 per update) is a legitimate non-stationarity device — it bounds effective sample size at 1/(1−γ) = 20, matching discounted-Bayesian bandit practice — but it is applied per-*update* rather than per-unit-time, and the companion `Confidence` formula (α+β)/(α+β+10) is an ad hoc saturation unrelated to posterior variance.

Decay is applied only when an update arrives (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/WeightedGrammar.fs:101-105`), so a rule untouched for a year retains full effective count and confidence, while frequently-updated rules forget fastest — the inverse of the intended recency semantics. The discounted-bandit literature applies the discount per time step across all arms, including un-pulled ones (Garivier & Moulines, 2008). The `Confidence` value n/(n+10) is consumed downstream — e.g., the max-blend at `v2/src/Tars.Evolution/GrammarMlBridge.fs:235` — as if it were a calibrated certainty, whereas the Beta posterior standard deviation √(αβ/((α+β)²(α+β+1))) is what those consumers implicitly assume. This finding carries medium confidence only because the framing (which half is "correct") is interpretive; the mechanics were confirmed by both review passes, including the steady-state fixed point c = 0.95c + 1 ⇒ c = 20.

### 12. The MCP replicator-dynamics tool is a provable no-op

**Claim** (`replicator-neutral-noop`, confidence: high, confirmed ×2). The MCP-exposed replicator-dynamics tool always runs with an empty outcome map, giving every species the neutral fitness 0.5, so the simulation provably preserves initial proportions: the tool's output is a no-op dressed as evolution.

`McpGrammarTools.fs:118` calls `ReplicatorDynamics.evolveEcosystem rules Map.empty`; `computeSpeciesFitness` returns 0.5 for empty outcome lists (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/ReplicatorDynamics.fs:75-86`), so fᵢ = f_avg for every species and the replicator step Δ = dt·xᵢ·(fᵢ − f_avg) = 0 (`v2/src/Tars.Evolution/ReplicatorDynamics.fs:112-127`). The fixed-point argument is exact whenever all proportions exceed the 0.01 smoothing floor, which holds for the live weights (0.19–0.37); the default 50 steps are therefore vacuous. The CLI path (`v2/src/Tars.Interface.Cli/Commands/GrammarCommand.fs:95`) does pass a non-empty outcome map, though review noted a caveat worth recording: those outcomes are synthesized from stored `SuccessRate`/`SelectionCount` — the code itself comments "dummy outcomes... no real outcome store yet" — so they are non-neutral but not raw execution records. The MCP/CLI contrast holds; neither surface currently runs replicator dynamics over real execution outcomes.

## Flagged (unverifiable, with caveats)

No claims were flagged. Every claim submitted to adversarial review was either confirmed against code anchors and live state or refuted; none rested on unverifiable evidence. Two minor evidence-level imprecisions surfaced during confirmation and are recorded inline above rather than flagged, since the substantive claims survived them: (i) `ResearchWeights.sampleMethod` is a live stochastic selector, narrowing Finding 4's "only stochastic selector" phrasing to the posterior-consuming paths; (ii) `CompressionRatio` has an ML-feature consumer in `GrammarMlBridge`, narrowing Finding 8's "no other consumer" to "no consumer that reaches a promotion decision."

## Refuted during review

None. All twelve claims survived both adversarial review passes with verdict *confirmed*; the dropped list is empty.

## Opportunities for TARS

Ranked by leverage — the first three restore statistical soundness to the existing machinery; the later ones connect the forces the architecture already names.

1. **Ground the observation signal in execution** (fixes Finding 2, the most corrosive defect). Route `PatternOutcomeStore` execution records (success + duration) into `WeightedGrammar.updateWeight` keyed by pattern id, and reserve `GrammarGovernor` decisions for gating only. Observation likelihood and decision policy must be separate; until they are, every downstream fix inherits a circular signal.

2. **Seed proper Beta priors and store (α, β) directly** (fixes Findings 1 and 10). Give every rule explicit pseudo-counts α₀ = `PriorSuccessRate`·c, β₀ = (1−`PriorSuccessRate`)·c with c ≈ 2, and persist (α, β) instead of round-tripping through (rate, count). This makes `PriorSuccessRate` live, removes the 0/1 collapse, and gives the ML bridge a correct injection point: convert ix predictions to pseudo-counts (α₀ += c·k·m, β₀ += c·k·(1−m)) so prediction strength and observation strength compose under later updates.

3. **Make the posterior reach the ranking, and restore exploration** (fixes Findings 3, 4, 5). Either rank in `classifyWeighted` by posterior mean directly or recompute `Weight` = f(posterior) after each update, with a regression test asserting that a rule with repeated failures drops in `classifyWeighted` order. Replace the mean-argmax in `banditScores`/`Recommend` with Thompson sampling over the already-available Beta(succ+1, fail+1) posteriors — this restores exploration for arms like ReAct (0/5) without hand-tuned floors and preserves the count information the softmax-of-means destroys.

4. **Convert the staircase into a compression-driven promotion test** (fixes Finding 8). Add an MDL gain criterion to the governor: promote a pattern one level only if (description length of traces without the abstraction) − (DL with the abstraction + DL of the abstraction itself) > 0, using the already-computed `CompressionRatio` and `TraceCount`. This turns the threshold staircase into DreamCoder-style library learning with the evidence TARS already produces.

5. **Couple Force 2 to Force 1 via per-nonterminal Dirichlet-multinomials** (fixes Finding 9). Introduce Dirichlet-multinomial counts keyed to EBNF alternation points in `wot.ebnf`, and export the resulting production probabilities as logit biases to the vLLM guided-decoding layer, so the probabilistic policy actually steers constrained decoding instead of living beside it.

6. **Repair the smaller mechanics.** Decay effective counts per evolve cycle across all rules (γ^Δt), not per update, and report posterior variance instead of n/(n+10) (Finding 11). Replace the cons-based running average with a stored (sum, count) or incremental mean (Finding 7). Fix or replace the false `bayesianAdjust` docstring; per-method Beta counters would make `sampleMethod` Thompson sampling for free (Finding 6). Load real outcomes inside the MCP replicator tool the way the CLI path does, and graduate both from synthesized to `PatternOutcomeStore`-backed outcomes (Finding 12).
